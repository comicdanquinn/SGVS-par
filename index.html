How SG vs Par Works

Par is the universal benchmark. The system does not compare you with a Tour player, scratch golfer, or handicap group. It measures how efficiently you used the strokes par gives you on each hole.

Every shot owns the position it creates. A poor shot is charged for leaving you in a difficult scoring position. The next shot is judged on how well it improves that inherited position.

Driving
Par-4 and par-5 tee shots. Good drives create an easier next scoring position; trouble, penalties, and forced recovery are charged here.
Approach
Par-3 tee shots and full shots toward the green. The better the position created, the more value the shot gains.
Short Game
Shots from 60 yards and in when off the green. Chips, pitches, and greenside bunker shots are scored by the position they leave.
Putting
Enter the starting putt distance and total putts. Routine two-putts from normal range are near neutral; short misses and three-putts lose strokes.

Why the Numbers Always Reconcile

Internally, each shot is scored from the change in Par Remaining:

Shot SGvP = PR before − PR after − 1

Because each shot picks up where the previous one left off, all of the shot values telescope together. The four categories always equal:

Par − Actual Score

So a birdie totals +1.0, par totals 0.0, bogey totals −1.0, and double bogey totals −2.0.

Simple Example — Par 4 Bogey

You hit a decent drive, miss the green with the approach, hit an average chip, and two-putt for 5.

The hole might break down as:

Driving 0.0 · Approach −0.5 · Short Game −0.5 · Putting 0.0 = −1.0

The system does not just tell you that you made bogey. It shows where the lost stroke came from.

Penalties

Penalty cost belongs to the shot that caused it. A drive into a penalty area is charged to Driving; an approach into a hazard is charged to Approach. Stroke-and-distance creates a −2.0 deficit before the replacement shot is evaluated.
