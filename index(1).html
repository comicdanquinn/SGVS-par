Quick Start: After every off-green shot, enter where the ball finished: the distance remaining and the one resulting position that best describes the shot — Fairway / Good Lie, Light Rough, Deep Rough, Bunker, Restricted, or Recovery Only. How SG vs Par Is Scored

SG vs Par measures how much of the work required to make par was created by each shot.

Par is the benchmark—not the distance of the shot. Distance is used to measure how much of the shot's assigned job was completed. Every shot inherits the position created by the shots before it.

Core accounting: 100% of one assigned job = 1.0 stroke of par created = 0.0 SGvP. 70% completed = −0.3. 50% = −0.5. Less than 10% = −1.0. Once a shot completes 100% of its own job, extra progress earns credit for completing part of the next par-job.

Putting: the finishing anchor

Putting is the absolute finishing ruler for the model. The ranges are based on the expected-putts curve rounded into 0.1 PR steps. PR 2.0 (26–35 ft) is the neutral two-putt position.

Par 3 — one full-shot job

The tee shot's job is the hole yardage minus the neutral two-putt distance (about 10–11 yards / 30–32 feet). Reaching the 26–35 ft putting range completes 100% of the full-shot job and is neutral. If only 70% of that job is completed, the tee shot is −0.3. Once the shot reaches the green inside the neutral range, the putting PR scale measures the extra par it created.

Neutral path: Tee/Approach → PR 2.0 → two putts = Par 3.

Par 4 — drive, approach, two putts

The neutral tee job is the established hole-relative driving percentage: 60–64.9% of the hole advanced = 0.0. The approach inherits whatever the drive created. Its neutral job is to move from that inherited position to the putting position corresponding to one full stroke of par created. From a neutral drive, that means reaching PR 2.0 / 26–35 ft.

Par 5 — divide the hole into two 50% jobs

A neutral Par-5 pathway is easiest to think of as two halves of the hole:

Shot 1: complete the first 50% of the hole.
Shots 2 + 3: together complete the remaining 50% of the hole, finishing at the neutral putting position PR 2.0 / about 26–35 ft rather than at the cup.

Example — 500-yard Par 5: Shot 1's neutral job is the first 250 yards. That leaves 250 yards for Shots 2 + 3 together to complete. How much of that remaining 50% each shot completes determines how much par each shot creates. The final full shot finishes the job at roughly 30–32 feet, then two neutral putts complete the Par 5.

Each shot inherits the SG vs Par position created by the shots before it. A previous loss is not reset by the next shot. If Shot 2 completes more of the remaining 50% than required for one neutral par-job, it begins completing Shot 3's job and can earn positive SGvP. If it completes less, Shot 3 inherits that unfinished work.

If a shot reaches the green, the putting PR scale becomes the finishing ruler. A better-than-neutral putting position can therefore create additional par value.

Neutral Par-5 path: Shot 1 completes 50% of the hole → Shots 2 + 3 complete the other 50% → two putts = Par 5.

Resulting Position Disadvantage

Only one disadvantage can apply. Choose the dominant problem the shot created. It reduces the amount of useful par created by that shot; disadvantages never stack.

Example: a shot that completes 100% of its advancement job but finishes Restricted creates only 0.5 strokes of useful par, so it scores −0.5 SGvP. The shot that created the problem owns the disadvantage.

The reconciliation rule

No matter how a hole is played, all shot values must reconcile to Par − Score: eagle +2, birdie +1, par 0, bogey −1, double −2.
